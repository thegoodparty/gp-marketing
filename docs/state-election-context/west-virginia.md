# West Virginia: state election context

Starting point for the location page editorial generator. This gives you the
structure that holds across West Virginia so you do not have to research the same
ground on every page. It is not an authority: per-page specifics, like which
offices are actually on this ballot and how many seats, come from our own race
data, which always beats anything inferred from here.

Source lines read: `citation · how universal · how verified`. See the
[README](./README.md) for what those mean. Every claim below has a verbatim quote
in [`research/west-virginia.json`](./research/west-virginia.json).

**The one thing to know first:** West Virginia's county offices are partisan and
elected in November of even-numbered years, after a party primary in May. School
boards and magistrates are nonpartisan and are decided outright at that **May
primary**, not in November. City and town elections are moving: a
2025 law requires every municipality to hold its elections on the statewide May
primary or November general election day, and charters with other dates have
until 1 July 2032 to comply. Until then, municipal election dates vary by place.

## Legislative calendar

Next regular session: convenes 13 January 2027 (the second Wednesday in January,
every year, W. Va. Const. art. VI, § 18). In 1973 and every fourth year after
(2025, 2029), the Legislature organizes in January and then recesses until the
second Wednesday in February; 2027 is not one of those years. A session may last
sixty calendar days unless two-thirds of each house extend it (art. VI, § 22), so
the 2027 session ends in mid-March. The governor must call a special session if
three fifths of each house apply in writing. During the session a bill the
governor does not return within five days (Sundays excepted) becomes law. After
the session adjourns, the governor has fifteen days (Sundays excepted) to file
objections, or the bill becomes law (art. VII, § 14), so the session's bills are
settled in early April.

New laws take effect ninety days after passage unless two-thirds of each house
vote otherwise (W. Va. Const. art. VI, § 30).

## How elections work in West Virginia

Safe on any West Virginia page, at the tier named.

### Election days

**County** offices are elected at the general election on the Tuesday after the
first Monday in November of even-numbered years. The party primary is the second
Tuesday in May of even-numbered years.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-1-31(a), 3-5-1 · statutory · read in statute*

**County boards of education and magistrates** are elected at the May primary, on
a nonpartisan ballot. That vote decides the seat: the top vote-getters are
certified elected, with no November round.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-6(a), (c), 3-5-6d(a)-(b) · statutory · read in statute*

Circuit judges are also elected on the nonpartisan May ballot, but if no candidate
in a division gets more than 30 percent, the top two meet again in November.

*W. Va. Code § 3-1-17(a), (d) · statutory · read in statute*

**Municipalities** must hold their elections together with a statewide primary or
general election, by agreement with the county commission. Charters that set
another day must change by 1 July 2032. So during the transition a city or town
may still vote on its old charter date. Take the date from race data, and never
state a city's election month from this doc.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-1-31(a), 8-5-5(a)-(b) · varies by place · read in statute*

**Watch out:** do not say West Virginia school board races are in November. They
are decided at the May primary.

### Primary type

Each party nominates its county candidates at the May primary, by plurality. A
primary voter receives their own party's ballot plus the nonpartisan ballots. The
statute says a voter registered as independent receives no party ballot, only the
nonpartisan ballots and questions. A candidate who loses a party primary may not
run for the same office as another party's candidate in November.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-4(a)-(b), (d), 3-1-35 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** do not call West Virginia primaries open. Avoid writing about
whether unaffiliated voters can choose a party ballot; the statute and party
practice may not line up, so leave it to race data and official voter guides.

### Partisanship

**County races are partisan**: commissioners, sheriff, prosecuting attorney,
assessor, surveyor and the county and circuit clerks are nominated in party
primaries.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-4(a), 3-1-17(b) · statutory · read in statute*

**School board, magistrate and circuit judge races are nonpartisan.** School board
members are elected without reference to party.

*W. Va. Code §§ 18-5-1, 3-5-6(a), 3-1-17(a) · statutory · read in statute*

**City and town races depend on the charter.** Each charter sets how candidates
are nominated and how municipal primaries and elections run, and general election
law fills any gaps. A municipality may even let local parties that are not
statewide parties take part in its primary. Never call a West Virginia city race
partisan or nonpartisan unless race data says which.

*W. Va. Code §§ 8-5-6, 3-1-8 · varies by place · read in statute*

**Watch out:** "West Virginia local races are partisan" is wrong for school
boards and magistrates. "Local races are nonpartisan" is wrong for every county
office.

### Winning an election

Plurality: primaries are won by a plurality, magistrate divisions go to the top
vote-getter, and multi-seat school board races go to the top vote-getters, subject
to the district limits below. The only runoff in the law read for this doc is the
circuit judge rule above. Do not mention runoffs for county, city or school races.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-4(b), 3-5-6(c), 3-5-6d(b), 3-1-17(d) · statutory · read in statute*

### Running as an independent

Groups with no party organization nominate candidates by **certificate**, for
candidates who are not already in the primary. Signers must be registered voters
living in the county, district or other division the office covers. The
certificate process does not apply to nonpartisan races, where every candidate
files without party.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-23(a), (c), 3-5-24(e) · statutory · read in statute*

## County government

West Virginia has 55 counties (our data has a page for each). Each has a **county
commission of three commissioners**. Commissioners are elected by the whole county
for six-year terms, staggered so one is elected every two years, and no two may
come from the same magisterial district. A candidate must live in the magisterial
district they seek by the filing deadline.

*W. Va. Const. art. IX, §§ 9, 10; W. Va. Code §§ 7-1-1(b), 7-1-1b(b) · statutory · read in statute*

A county may ask the Legislature to reform its commission, with voter approval.
A county council created this way since 2008 has four or more members. Take the
governing body from race data if it is not a three-member commission.

*W. Va. Const. art. IX, § 13; W. Va. Code § 7-1-1(c) · varies by place · read in statute*

Counties also elect, every four years, a **sheriff** (limited to two consecutive
terms), **prosecuting attorney, surveyor of lands** and **one or two
assessors**, and every six years a **county clerk** and a **circuit clerk**.

*W. Va. Const. art. IX, §§ 1, 3, 12; W. Va. Code § 3-1-17(b) · statutory · read in statute*

## City, town and village government

West Virginia classifies municipalities by population, and class changes
automatically with the census.

*W. Va. Code § 8-1-3 · statutory · read in statute*

- **Class I city:** over 50,000.
- **Class II city:** over 10,000 to 50,000.
- **Class III city:** over 2,000 to 10,000.
- **Class IV town or village:** 2,000 or fewer.

*W. Va. Code § 8-1-3 · statutory · read in statute*

Unless the charter says otherwise, a municipality elects a **mayor, a recorder and
council members**, who together form the governing body. Without wards there are
at least five council members. Our pages label the recorder race "Town Recorder"
or "City Clerk/Ward Officer". Towns and villages may move to four-year terms by
vote, and any municipality may stagger or change terms by ordinance.

*W. Va. Code §§ 8-5-7(a)-(b), 8-5-5(d)-(e) · usual case · read in statute*

### Home rule and charters

Municipalities over 2,000 people may frame and adopt their own charters, but a
charter or ordinance that conflicts with general law is void. The state's
Municipal Home Rule Program is permanent, and state election administration and
offense rules apply to every municipal election even under a home rule charter.

*W. Va. Const. art. VI, § 39a; W. Va. Code §§ 8-1-5a(b), 3-1-2a(a) · statutory · read in statute*

## School districts

Each of our 55 West Virginia school district pages is a county school district.
Each county board of education has five members, elected at the May primary
without party for four-year terms, and no more than two members may come from the
same magisterial district.

*W. Va. Code §§ 18-5-1, 3-5-6(a)-(b) · statutory · read in statute*

### Magistrate pages

Every county page carries a "County Court Judge" race, which is a magistrate
seat. In West Virginia "county court" is the old name for the county commission,
so never call these races "county court" in copy. Call them magistrate races,
nonpartisan, decided at the May primary.

*W. Va. Code §§ 3-5-6d(a); W. Va. Const. art. IX, § 9 · statutory · read in statute*

## Writing rules

These are the ways West Virginia copy goes visibly wrong.

1. **School board and magistrate races are decided at the May primary.** Never
   say they are on the November ballot.
2. **Do not state a city's election month.** Municipal dates are moving to the
   statewide dates by 2032 and vary until then.
3. **County races are partisan; school board and magistrate races are not.** City
   races depend on the charter.
4. Do not call West Virginia primaries open.
5. Do not mention runoffs for local races. Plurality wins.
6. Do not call a commission race a "county court" race, or a magistrate race a
   county court race.
7. No two county commissioners, and no more than two school board members, may
   come from one magisterial district. Do not describe a seat as representing a
   district unless race data says so; commissioners are elected countywide.
8. Never name an officeholder or a candidate.
9. Avoid anything that changes within a year: who has filed, whether a race is
   contested, filing deadlines, current ballot measures.
