# Idaho: state election context

Starting point for the location page editorial generator. This gives you the
structure that holds across Idaho so you do not have to research the same ground
on every page. It is not an authority: per-page specifics, like which offices are
actually on this ballot and how many seats, come from our own race data, which
always beats anything inferred from here.

Source lines read: `citation · how universal · how verified`. See the
[README](./README.md) for what those mean. Every claim below has a verbatim quote
in [`research/idaho.json`](./research/idaho.json).

**The one thing to know first:** Idaho splits cleanly by tier. County offices are
partisan and elected in November of even-numbered years, after a closed party
primary in May. Cities, school boards and highway districts are nonpartisan, and
cities and school boards vote in November of odd-numbered years. Every Idaho
county uses the same structure: three commissioners and a fixed set of elected
officers, and the law read for this doc gives no home rule charter route to
change it.

## Legislative calendar

Next regular session: convenes 11 January 2027 (the second Monday in January,
every year, Idaho Const. art. III, § 8(1)). There is no fixed length. The governor
can call an extraordinary session, and legislative leaders must call a special
session when 60% of each house petitions for one. During the session a bill the
governor does not return within five days (Sundays excepted) becomes law. After
adjournment the governor has ten days (Sundays excepted) to file a veto, or the
bill becomes law (Idaho Const. art. IV, § 10), so the last bills are settled
about two weeks after the session ends.

New laws take effect on 1 July or sixty days after the session ends, whichever is
later, unless the act declares an emergency (Idaho Code § 67-510; Idaho Const.
art. III, § 22).

## How elections work in Idaho

Safe on any Idaho page, at the tier named.

### Election days

**County** offices are elected at the general election on the first Tuesday after
the first Monday in November of even-numbered years. The party primary is the
third Tuesday of May in even-numbered years.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-101, 34-102 · statutory · read in statute*

**Cities** hold their general election on the Tuesday after the first Monday in
November of odd-numbered years, choosing mayors and council members.

*Idaho Code §§ 50-402(a), 50-405(1) · statutory · read in statute*

**School trustees** are elected on the same day: the Tuesday after the first
Monday in November of odd-numbered years.

*Idaho Code § 33-503(1) · statutory · read in statute*

**Only two election dates a year.** No county may hold more than two elections a
year: the third Tuesday in May and the Tuesday after the first Monday in
November (plus emergencies). Special districts (fire, cemetery, library,
recreation, water and sewer and others) vote on one of those two dates, set by
each district's own law. A district that voted on another day before 2011 moved
to the nearest permitted date. Water districts are exempt.

*Idaho Code § 34-106(1), (4), (6) · varies by place · read in statute*

The county clerk runs elections for every city, school district, highway district
and special district. Irrigation districts and several water-related districts
run their own.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-1401(1), 50-403 · statutory · read in statute*

**Uncontested city races are not held.** In a city under 100,000 people, when
only one candidate files for mayor or a council seat (or no more candidates than
seats), there is no election and the candidate is declared elected.

*Idaho Code § 50-405(2) · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** Idaho city and school board elections are in odd-numbered years.
Never put an Idaho county race in an odd year or a city race in an even year.

### Primary type

Idaho's party primaries are **closed by default**. Each party has its own primary
ballot, and a voter registered with a party may vote only in that party's
primary. A party may open its primary to unaffiliated voters, or to members of
named other parties, by notifying the secretary of state by the last Tuesday in
November before the primary. An unaffiliated voter who picks a party's primary
declares it at the polls, and the county clerk records the choice in the voter's
history.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-904(1), 34-904A(1)-(3) · statutory · read in statute*

Party candidates must be affiliated with their party when they file, and a
candidate who loses a primary may not run in November under another party or as
an independent.

*Idaho Code § 34-704(1), (4) · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** do not call Idaho primaries open. Do not say unaffiliated voters
can never vote in one either: a party may allow it.

### Partisanship

**County races are partisan.** County candidates file a party declaration and
are nominated in the May primary, or run as independents.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-704(3), 34-708(2) · statutory · read in statute*

**City races are nonpartisan.** City candidates are nominated by a declaration
that may not mention a party.

*Idaho Code § 50-406 · statutory · read in statute*

**School trustee and special district races are nonpartisan.** Candidates are
nominated by petitions signed by electors of their zone or district.

*Idaho Code §§ 33-502, 34-1404(1) · statutory · read in statute*

**Highway district commissioners are elected on a nonpartisan ballot**, except in
a single countywide highway district, where candidates file with the county clerk
like party candidates. A 2026 law making that exception explicit takes effect on
1 January 2027.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-905A, 34-704(3) · varies by place · read in statute*

**Watch out:** "Idaho local races are partisan" is wrong for cities, schools and
districts, and "nonpartisan" is wrong for every county office.

### Winning an election

Plurality everywhere: county, city, school trustee and highway district races go
to the candidate with the most votes. No local runoff was found in the law read
for this doc. Multi-seat city races go to the top vote-getters.

*Idaho Code §§ 34-1208, 34-1209, 50-412, 33-503(1), 34-905A · statutory · read in statute*

### Running as an independent

For **county** office, an independent files a declaration stating no party
affiliation, with a nominating petition, during the same filing period as party
candidates. Independents do not appear on the primary ballot; they go straight
to November.

*Idaho Code § 34-708(1)-(2) · statutory · read in statute*

For **city, school and district** offices there is no independent route because
there are no party nominees. Every candidate files the same way.

*Idaho Code §§ 50-406, 34-1404(1) · statutory · read in statute*

## County government

Idaho's counties are those recognized at statehood, and our data has 44 county
pages. County government must be uniform across the state.

*Idaho Const. art. XVIII, §§ 1, 5 · statutory · read in statute*

Every county has a **board of three county commissioners**. The county is split
into three commissioner districts, and each commissioner must have lived in their
district for 90 days before the primary. Terms alternate: at each even-year
election one seat is filled for two years and one for four years, rotating among
the districts. So every county has commission races every two years.

*Idaho Const. art. XVIII, § 10; Idaho Code §§ 31-701, 31-703, 31-704, 34-617(2) · statutory · read in statute*

Every county also elects, for four-year terms, a **sheriff, assessor, coroner,
treasurer** (who is also public administrator and tax collector), **clerk of the
district court** (also auditor and recorder) and **prosecuting attorney**. The
constitution says no other county offices may be established.

*Idaho Const. art. XVIII, § 6; Idaho Code §§ 31-2001, 34-619(1), 34-623(1) · statutory · read in statute*

### Home rule and charters

The law read for this doc provides no home rule charter process for counties or
cities. Counties and cities may make local police, sanitary and other regulations that do not conflict with
general law, and the legislature organizes both by general law. So the
structures above hold in every county, and election rules are flat-assertable.

*Idaho Const. art. XII, §§ 1, 2; art. XVIII, § 5 · statutory · read in statute*

## City government

Idaho incorporates places as **cities**. A new city needs at least 125 qualified
electors. Our Idaho data has no towns, villages or townships.

*Idaho Const. art. XII, § 1; Idaho Code § 50-101 · statutory · read in statute*

The default structure is a **mayor and a council of four or six members**, half
the council elected at each odd-year city election. Mayors and council members
serve four-year terms. The mayor is the chief administrative official and votes
only to break a tie.

*Idaho Code §§ 50-601, 50-602, 50-701, 50-702 · usual case · read in statute*

Local variations:

- A city may adopt the **council-manager plan** by vote, which changes the
  council and the mayor's role.
- A city may switch between four and six council members by vote.
- A city may number its council seats, so candidates run for a specific seat.
- A city under 100,000 people may elect its council by district. **A city over
  100,000 must.**

*Idaho Code §§ 50-801, 50-703(1), 50-707, 50-707A(1) · varies by place · read in statute*

A city in more than one county has its elections run jointly by the county
clerks.

*Idaho Code § 50-403(2) · statutory · read in statute*

## School districts

113 of our 362 Idaho pages are school districts. Most are named with a district
number, and "joint" districts cross county lines, with the clerk of the home
county coordinating their elections. Elementary-only
districts have three trustees and other districts five, with some exceptions
after consolidations or boundary changes. Trustees serve four-year terms and must
live in the trustee zone they represent; each zone elects its own trustee.

*Idaho Code §§ 33-501(1)-(2), 33-503(1), 34-1401(1) · usual case · read in statute*

## Metro and major-place exceptions

### Boise and other cities over 100,000

`id/ada-county/boise`, `id/ada-county/meridian`

A city over 100,000 people must elect its council by district. Uncontested races
in these cities still go on the ballot, because the cancellation rule applies
only below 100,000. Take the number of seats from race data.

*Idaho Code §§ 50-707A(1), 50-405(2) · statutory · read in statute*

### Countywide highway districts

`id/ada-county` (its "County Highway District Commission" race)

A single countywide highway district's commissioners file with the county clerk
like party candidates, unlike other highway districts. Take partisanship from
race data.

## Writing rules

These are the ways Idaho copy goes visibly wrong.

1. **Never infer a county from a place name.** Three of our Idaho place pages
   share a name with a different Idaho county: **Kootenai** is in Bonner County,
   **Teton** is in Fremont County and **Shoshone** is in Lincoln County. The full
   list is in
   [`research/places-named-after-another-county.json`](./research/places-named-after-another-county.json).
   Always take the county from the page data.
2. **Use the right calendar for the tier.** Counties: November of even years,
   May primary. Cities and school boards: November of odd years. Special
   districts: May or November, depending on the district.
3. **Partisanship follows the tier.** County races are partisan. City, school
   board, highway district and special district races are nonpartisan.
4. Do not call Idaho primaries open.
5. Do not mention runoffs. Plurality wins.
6. Every county has exactly three commissioners. Do not describe a county
   executive, a larger commission or a county charter.
7. Do not call any Idaho place a town, village or township, and do not mention
   townships at all, even where our position labels say "township".
8. Never name an officeholder or a candidate.
9. Avoid anything that changes within a year: who has filed, whether a race is
   contested, filing deadlines, current ballot measures.
