# North Dakota: state election context

Starting point for the location page editorial generator. This gives you the
structure that holds across North Dakota so you do not have to research the same
ground on every page. It is not an authority: per-page specifics, like which
offices are actually on this ballot and how many seats, come from our own race
data, which always beats anything inferred from here.

Source lines read: `citation · how universal · how verified`. See the
[README](./README.md) for what those mean.

**The one thing to know first:** every local office in North Dakota is
nonpartisan by statute, **county offices included**. County candidates run on a
separate "no-party" ballot. Cities vote in **June** of even years, not November,
and townships vote at an annual meeting in **March**. North Dakota also has **no
voter registration**, so copy must never tell a reader to register.

## How elections work in North Dakota

Safe on any North Dakota page, at any tier.

### No voter registration

North Dakota does not register voters. The secretary of state keeps a central
voter file, but state law says the voter-list procedure may not be used to
require registration. A voter must be a citizen, at least 18, and have lived in
the precinct for 30 days, and must show identification to vote.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-01-04, 16.1-01-05.1, 16.1-02-01 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** never write "register to vote", "registered voters" or a
registration deadline for a North Dakota page. Say "eligible voters" or
"residents who are eligible to vote".

### Nonpartisan at every local level

**County.** No party may be named in the petition of a candidate for any
elective county office, and those candidates appear on a separate **no-party
ballot** without a party designation. Partisan nominations may not be made for
these offices. The same rule covers supreme court and district court judges.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-11-08, 16.1-11-24, 16.1-11-37, 16.1-06-08 · statutory · read in statute*

**City.** No party may be named in the petition of a candidate for any city
office, and the city auditor puts only the candidates' names on the ballot.
Elected park district boards are chosen at the same city elections.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-21-06, 40-21-08, 40-49-07 · statutory · read in statute*

**Township.** Officers are elected by ballot at the annual township meeting. The
ballot carries only the names voted for and the offices.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 58-04-10, 58-04-11 · statutory · read in statute*

**School district.** The ballot must be nonpartisan in form.

*N.D.C.C. § 15.1-09-11 · statutory · read in statute*

**Special districts.** Soil conservation district supervisors and Southwest
Water Authority directors are both on the no-party ballot.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 4.1-20-16, 61-24.5-06 · statutory · read in statute*

Partisan races in North Dakota are federal, statewide and legislative. Those are
the offices on the party sections of the primary ballot.

*N.D.C.C. § 16.1-11-26 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** in many states county offices are partisan. Not here. Never
attach a party to a North Dakota county
commissioner, sheriff, state's attorney, auditor, treasurer, recorder, mayor,
council member, city commissioner, park commissioner, township supervisor,
school board member or judge.

### Election days, by tier

**County.** County offices follow the state cycle: a primary on the **second
Tuesday in June** of even-numbered years, then the general election on the first
Tuesday after the first Monday in **November** of even-numbered years. Counties
cannot move these dates, and a home rule charter cannot change county election
law.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-11-01, 16.1-13-01, 16.1-13-02, 11-09.1-04 · statutory · read in statute*

**City.** City elections are held on the **second Tuesday in June of
even-numbered years**, the same day as the state primary. A city must agree with
its county on running the election together. There is no November city election
and no city primary: candidates are nominated by petition and elected in June.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-21-02, 40-21-07 · statutory · read in statute*

A city cannot move the date. A home rule charter overrides state law on most
local matters but **not on city elections**; the only election rule a charter
can change is the number of signatures a city candidate needs.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-05.1-05, 40-05.1-06(9) · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** many sources assume local races are in November. In North Dakota,
city, park district and (usually) school races are decided in June. Never say a
North Dakota city votes in November or in an odd year.

**Township.** Township electors hold an annual meeting in **March** and elect
officers there by ballot. The law sets the month, not a day.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 58-04-01, 58-04-10 · statutory · read in statute*

**School district.** Each school board holds an election **every year**,
between 1 April and 30 June. A board may combine its election with the city's
June election.

*N.D.C.C. § 15.1-09-22 · varies by place · read in statute*

**Special districts.** Park district commissioners are elected at the regular
city election in June. Soil conservation district supervisors are elected at the
November general election. Southwest Water Authority directors are elected on
the no-party primary ballot in June, and the directors for Dickinson and Mandan
at the city election.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-49-07, 4.1-20-17, 61-24.5-06, 61-24.5-07 · statutory · read in statute*

### Primaries

**State and county.** The June primary uses one consolidated ballot. It has a
section for each party plus a separate no-party ballot and any measures. A voter
asks for no party ballot and declares no party: the voter picks one party's
section on the spot. Votes in more than one party's section void the party
ballot, but the voter's no-party and measure votes still count.

*N.D.C.C. § 16.1-11-22 · statutory · read in statute*

On the **no-party** part, the top vote-getters, up to twice the number of seats,
advance to November. A candidate must also win at least as many votes as the
petition signatures the office requires.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-11-36, 16.1-11-37 · statutory · read in statute*

So a county race with only one or two candidates per seat still appears on the
June ballot, and the real contest is in November. Do not describe a county
primary as a party primary.

**City, park and school.** No primary. Candidates file and the most votes wins
on election day.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-21-07, 40-21-13, 15.1-09-15 · statutory · read in statute*

### Winning

Plurality. Unless a law says otherwise, the candidate with the most votes is
elected. There is no runoff in state law.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-01-06, 58-04-14, 15.1-09-15 · statutory · read in statute*

**Approval voting and ranked-choice voting are banned** for nominating or
electing anyone to local, state or federal office, and a home rule ordinance
that conflicts with the ban is void.

*N.D.C.C. § 16.1-01-06.1 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** searches still turn up pages about Fargo's former approval-voting
system. It no longer applies. Never describe approval or ranked-choice voting
for any North Dakota race.

### Getting on the ballot

County candidates file a petition with the county auditor; city candidates file
one with the city auditor; school candidates file a signed statement of
candidacy with the school district. Only qualified electors of the place may
sign a nominating petition. For a city office elected by ward, signers must live
in the ward.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-11-11, 16.1-11-16, 40-21-07, 15.1-09-08 · statutory · read in statute*

A city candidate may run for only one city office at an election (mayor or
council, not both).

*N.D.C.C. § 40-21-07 · statutory · read in statute*

### Running as an independent

This applies only to offices on the party ballot: federal, statewide and
legislative. Local offices are all nonpartisan, so "independent" has no meaning
for them.

An independent skips the primary and gets on the November ballot with a
**certificate of nomination by petition**. The route is open for any office
except those on the no-party ballot, and the name appears as an independent
nomination.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-12-01, 16.1-12-02 · statutory · read in statute*

Who may sign: only qualified electors of the district. A voter may sign
independent petitions for more than one candidate for the same office, and
nothing bars a primary voter from signing.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 16.1-11-16, 16.1-12-03 · statutory · read in statute*

A candidate who lost the primary cannot then appear on the November ballot for
the same office.

*N.D.C.C. § 16.1-13-06 · statutory · read in statute*

## County government

North Dakota has **53 counties**, and we have one page for each. Every one has a
board of county commissioners until it adopts an optional form, and there is no
independent city or consolidated city-county.

*N.D.C.C. § 11-01-01; N.D. Const. art. VII, § 7 · statutory · read in statute*

### The county commission

A **board of county commissioners** of three or five members, on four-year
terms. Each commissioner is elected by the district where the commissioner
lives, unless 60 percent of county voters have chosen at-large elections.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 11-11-01, 11-11-02, 11-11-03, 11-07-06 · usual case · read in statute*

**Watch out:** never state whether a named county has three or five
commissioners, or whether they are elected by district or at large.

### Other elected county offices

By default a county elects an auditor, recorder, treasurer, state's attorney and
sheriff, every four years. In counties of 6,000 or fewer the recorder also
serves as clerk of the district court. Every elective county office has a
four-year term under the constitution.

*N.D.C.C. § 11-10-02; N.D. Const. art. VII, § 8 · usual case · read in statute*

The sheriff must be elected. Everything else can change by local vote: a county
may combine, split or make appointive any elective office except sheriff, voters
may make the state's attorney appointive, and counties may share a combined
office with other counties. The coroner is appointed.

*N.D. Const. art. VII, § 8; N.D.C.C. §§ 11-10.2-01, 11-10-02.3, 11-10.3-01, 11-19.1-03 · varies by place · read in statute*

Our race data reflects this: all 53 county pages carry a sheriff and a
commission, but only 34 carry an auditor, 32 a treasurer and 35 a recorder, and
several carry combined offices (auditor-treasurer, recorder-clerk). Name only
the county offices race data shows.

**Watch out:** the prosecutor is the **state's attorney**, not the district
attorney or county attorney. Our position label says "district attorney" on 51
county pages and "county attorney" on one (Steele County). Write state's
attorney.

### Charters and optional forms

Counties may adopt a home rule charter by popular vote, a county manager form
(the manager may be appointed or elected) or the consolidated office form.

*N.D. Const. art. VII, §§ 6, 7; N.D.C.C. §§ 11-09.1-04, 11-09-01, 11-08-01 · statutory · read in statute*

Home rule does **not** reach county elections. A county charter supersedes state
law on county matters "except for matters pertaining to county elections", and
any home rule ordinance on county elections that conflicts with state law is
void. A charter also cannot make the sheriff or state's attorney appointive, or
change township government without the township's consent.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 11-09.1-04, 11-09.1-05(6) · statutory · read in statute*

None of this changes partisanship or timing: county offices stay on the no-party
ballot, in June and November of even years. Which counties have charters is not
something we hold, so do not say a named county is a home rule county.

### Townships

North Dakota has **civil townships**. Many rural areas are organized into one;
others are not. A township exists only after a petition and a vote of its
residents, and voters can later dissolve it. Where no township is organized,
there is no township government or township election. Township law never applies
inside a city.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 58-01-02, 58-02-01, 58-02-28 · statutory · read in statute*

An organized township elects a board of **three supervisors** (voters may expand
it to five), each on a three-year term with one seat up each year on a
three-member board, plus a clerk, a treasurer and an assessor on two-year terms.
Voters may merge the clerk and treasurer or make the assessor appointive. All are
elected at the March meeting by voters who are eligible at general elections. If
a township fails to call its meeting, the county commission appoints the
officers.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 58-05-02, 58-04-02.1, 58-04-08, 58-04-20 · usual case · read in statute*

**Watch out:** the office is **township supervisor**. Our data labels the Grand
Forks County township seats "township trustee" (41 positions); the Cass and Ward
county seats are labeled "township supervisor" (53). Copy should say supervisor
regardless of the label, and must never call a township a city.

Our data carries township offices on only 94 pages, all in Cass, Grand Forks and
Ward counties. Most organized townships elsewhere in the state have no page.

## City government

### One kind of municipality: the city

Every incorporated place in North Dakota is a **city**. In state law a
municipality is a city and nothing else; there are no villages, towns or
boroughs. Size does not create classes. What differs is the form of government.

*N.D.C.C. § 40-01-01 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** never call a North Dakota place a town, village or borough, even
a very small one. "New Town" (`nd/mountrail-county/new-town`) is a city whose
name happens to include the word.

### Council cities

A city council made up of the **mayor and council members**, at least three
council members. Cities of 600 people or fewer elect the council at large; larger
council cities elect by ward, except that a city of up to 5,000 may choose
at-large elections by ordinance. Council members serve four years, with about
half elected at each election. The mayor serves four years. The city auditor and
most other officers are appointed, not elected.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-08-01, 40-08-03, 40-08-04, 40-08-04.2, 40-08-06, 40-08-14, 40-14-04 · usual case · read in statute*

### Commission cities

A **board of city commissioners**: a president of the board and at least three
commissioners, all elected at large, on four-year terms with about half elected
every two years. A candidate runs for president or commissioner, not both.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-01-01, 40-09-01, 40-09-03, 40-09-04 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** a commission city has no mayor in law. The presiding officer is
the president of the board of city commissioners. Our data labels this seat "president of council" on 10 pages,
including Bismarck and Williston. Use the office title race data shows and never
add a mayor that is not on the page.

### Modern council cities and city managers

The **modern council** form has a council of at least four, one of whom is the
mayor, elected at large or by ward to four-year terms. Any city may also adopt a
city manager plan, in which the manager is appointed.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-04.1-01, 40-10 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** which form a city uses is not published anywhere we read. Do not
name the form for a named city, and do not state a council size or whether seats
are by ward.

### Size and terms

Voters can enlarge or shrink a city governing body. Terms are set by statute at
four years for mayor, council, commission, park board and municipal judge.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-06-09, 40-08-06, 40-09-04, 40-49-05, 40-18.1-05 · statutory · read in statute*

### Home rule cities

Any city may adopt a home rule charter; there is no population threshold. A
charter can reshape the city's form of government and officers, but it cannot
change city election law, except for the number of signatures a city candidate
needs.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-05.1-01, 40-05.1-05, 40-05.1-06 · statutory · read in statute*

So in North Dakota, unlike Minnesota, a home rule city still votes in June of
even years, still has nonpartisan races and still elects by plurality.

### Municipal judges and park districts

A city that sets up a municipal court must elect at least one judge, for four
years. Our data carries a municipal judge on 42 city pages, labeled "local court
judge".

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-18.1-01, 40-18.1-05 · statutory · read in statute*

A city may create a **park district** ("park district of the city of ..."),
governed by an elected board of five or three park commissioners on four-year
terms, elected at the regular city election.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 40-49-04, 40-49-05, 40-49-07 · statutory · read in statute*

**Watch out:** in our data the park board is often filed on a separate page, not
the city's own. See "Same-name places" below.

## School and special districts

These are part of the third election tier described above.

A school board has **five, seven or nine** members on three-year terms, elected
every year between April and June on a nonpartisan ballot; the most votes wins.
We have 170 school district pages.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 15.1-09-01, 15.1-09-02, 15.1-09-11, 15.1-09-15, 15.1-09-22 · varies by place · read in statute*

**Soil conservation district** supervisors are elected at the November general
election on the no-party ballot to six-year terms. Our data has a soil
conservation board on 80 county pages and 3 others.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 4.1-20-16, 4.1-20-17, 4.1-20-18 · statutory · read in statute*

The **Southwest Water Authority** covers Adams, Billings, Bowman, Dunn, Golden
Valley, Grant, Hettinger, Mercer, Morton, Slope and Stark counties, and adjoining
counties may join. It elects one director per county plus two from Dickinson and
one from Mandan. The "water district board" on 12 of our southwestern county
pages (those eleven plus Oliver) is this board.

*N.D.C.C. §§ 61-24.5-03, 61-24.5-04 · statutory · read in statute*

## Writing rules

These are the ways North Dakota copy goes visibly wrong.

1. **Never say a North Dakota local race is partisan**, and never attach a party
   to a county, city, park, township, school or special district candidate.
   County races here are nonpartisan by law.
2. **Never infer a county from a place name.** 7 North Dakota municipal pages
   are named after a county they are not in: Hettinger is in Adams County,
   McHenry in Foster County, Steele in Kidder County, Towner in McHenry County,
   Mercer in McLean County, Golden Valley in Mercer County and Adams in Walsh
   County. Always take the county from the page data.
3. **Watch for same-name places.** 77 groups of our North Dakota pages share a
   name inside one county. Most are a city and a second page whose slug ends in
   `-city` (43) or `-township` (31). The bare slug is usually the city. The
   `-city` page usually holds only the **park district** board, and sometimes a
   municipal judge or **township** offices (`cass-county/casselton-city`,
   `cass-county/harwood-city`, `grand-forks-county/grand-forks-city`). A few
   `-township` pages hold only a park board or school board. Decide what a page
   is from the offices on it, never from the slug.
4. **Never mention voter registration.** North Dakota has none. Say "eligible
   voters".
5. **Get the month right.** County: June primary, November general, even years.
   City and park: June of even years. Township: March, every year. School: every
   year, April to June. Never say a city votes in November.
6. **Call the prosecutor the state's attorney** and township board members
   **supervisors**, whatever the position label says.
7. Never call a North Dakota place a town, village or borough. Never add a mayor
   to a commission city page that does not show one.
8. Do not state a commission or council size, whether seats are by district or
   ward, which form of government a city uses, whether a county or city has a
   home rule charter, or which county offices are elected beyond what race data
   shows. All vary by place.
9. Never describe a runoff, ranked-choice voting or approval voting. All races
   are plurality, and the last two are banned by law.
10. Never name an officeholder or a candidate.
11. Avoid anything that changes within a year: who has filed, whether a race is
    contested, filing deadlines, current ballot measures.

## Data issues

Logged for the data team. Never write copy around them.

- **Township races on `-city` pages.** `cass-county/casselton-city`,
  `cass-county/ayr-city`, `cass-county/gardner-city` and
  `cass-county/harwood-city` carry only township offices. `cass-county/davenport-city`,
  `ward-county/surrey-city` and `grand-forks-county/larimore-city` mix city and
  township offices on one page. `grand-forks-county/grand-forks-city`,
  `grand-forks-county/northwood-city` and `grand-forks-county/inkster-city`
  carry township trustee races. This looks like the Census "X city" subdivision
  problem seen in South Dakota.
- **Park district boards on separate pages.** 58 municipal pages hold nothing
  but a park district board, mostly `-city` twins of a real city page
  (`burleigh-county/bismarck-city`, `ward-county/minot-city`), plus some
  `-township` pages (`richland-county/wyndmere-township`). Five county pages also
  carry a park board.
- **School boards on township pages.** `ward-county/afton-township`,
  `freedom-township`, `gasman-township`, `torning-township` and
  `grand-forks-county/johnstown-township`, `mekinock-township` carry a school
  board position.
- **Labels.** "township trustee" in Grand Forks County (should be supervisor);
  "district attorney" on 51 county pages and "county attorney" on Steele County
  (the office is state's attorney); "president of council" for what is
  probably the president of a board of city commissioners.
